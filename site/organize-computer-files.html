How to organize files and folders on your computer

You do not need a complicated system. A few folders, sensible names and ten minutes a month keep a computer tidy. Here is a setup that works on Windows and Mac.

1. Use a few top-level folders

Keep everything under the folders your computer already has, and add only what you need:

  • Documents for anything you wrote or need to keep: split into Work, Personal, Money, Home.
  • Pictures for photos, one folder per year, then per month or event.
  • Downloads as a waiting room, not storage.
  • Desktop for what you are working on this week, nothing else.

Go two or three levels deep at most. If you have to click through five folders to reach a file, the system is too detailed to keep up.

2. Name files so they sort themselves

  • Start with the date as year-month-day when time matters: 2026-11-03 electricity bill.pdf. Files then line up in order.
  • Say what it is, not "final" or "new": lease agreement signed.pdf beats scan_final2.pdf.
  • Keep the same pattern for the same kind of file.

3. Clear the backlog in one go

The hard part is the pile you already have: hundreds of loose files in Downloads, on the Desktop or in an old "stuff" folder. Sorting those by hand is why most people never start.

Once the pile is split into a few folders, moving each folder to where it belongs takes minutes.

4. Organize the desktop

  • Mac: right-click the desktop and choose Use Stacks. Files are grouped by kind automatically.
  • Windows: right-click the desktop, choose View, then Auto arrange icons. To group files, put them in a folder and use Group by, Type.

Stacks and grouping only tidy the view. To clear the desktop for real, move the files into a folder and sort that folder.

5. Ten minutes a month

  • Sort Downloads again and move the keepers out. See organizing the Downloads folder.
  • Empty the desktop into the right folders.
  • Delete installers and zip files you have already used.
  • Check that your backup ran. Organized files that are not backed up are still at risk.
